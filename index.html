SAMA research recording

A short video of your face while you sit, talk and count, used to study whether ordinary webcams can measure heart rate and heart-rate variability for people of every skin tone. Everything below happens in your own browser on your own computer or phone.

What this involves

  • About 8–10 minutes, start to finish, at a time and place you choose.
  • Your browser will ask permission to use your camera. We record video only, no audio and no microphone.
  • You will be asked to sit still, then talk and count out loud while looking at the screen, following on-screen prompts — nobody else needs to be present or watching.
  • At the end, two short questions about your skin tone (so results can be checked across different skin tones, which is the whole point of the study).
  • No diagnosis, health reading or medical advice is given to you at any point.
  • If you are online, the recording uploads automatically when you finish. If you are not, it is saved only on this device until you are — nothing is lost either way.
  • You may stop at any point before finishing by closing this tab; nothing already recorded is kept unless the final upload/save step completed.

Your data

  • Stored under a random participant code, not your name.
  • Used only for this research project; not shared outside the research team.
  • You can ask for your recording to be deleted later by contacting the researcher (email below) with your participant code, which is shown to you at the end.

Before you start

  • Sit about an arm's length from the screen, with your face centred in the picture above.
  • Using a mobile phone? Prop it up on a stable surface (a stack of books, a stand) so it doesn't move — please don't hold it in your hand. A laptop or desktop webcam is just as good.
  • Try to keep your head roughly still during each step — small movements are fine.
  • The recording runs straight through, about minutes, with on-screen instructions for each part. You don't need to do anything except follow them.
  • Please don't close this tab once you start — if you need to stop, that's okay, just know the recording won't be saved unless you reach the end.
